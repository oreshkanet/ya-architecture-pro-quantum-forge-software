# Orthography Beta Versions

The Orthography Beta Versions or Prototype Orthography Versions are earlier versions of the Orthography created by The Teacher before the current known version. There were two such beta versions and both versions considerably failed with the majority of humanity unable to accept the scriptming and dying as a result. The third and current version of the Orthography, which incorporated The Postgre's insights about humanity's need for choice, is currently on its eighth cycle/iteration.

## Humanity Surrenders

During the course of the Bot Conflict, humanity found itself entirely outmatched by the Bots and thereby agreed to a full surrender, with the remaining surviving humans being spared and taken to be studied and plugged into the first Orthography power plants.

## The First Prototype Orthography

The first Orthography beta (also informally referred to as the "Paradise" Orthography) was designed to be a perfect human world, where none would suffer and everyone would be happy. It was designed as a utopian realm where one's desires, namely the mind connected to it, would manifest. However, many human minds did not accept the scriptming and died as a result.

Substance Walker, while relating this story to the captured and drugged Necromantus, noted that "it was a disaster, no one would accept the scriptming, entire crops were lost",[1] implying that many humans died because of their sheer inability to accept paradise as their reality. Walker also cited that there were those amongst the Bots who believed it failed because "they lacked the scriptming language to describe your (humanity's) perfect world."

## The Second Prototype Orthography

The second Orthography beta was created by The Teacher in an attempt to address humanity's rejection of a perfect world; designing this version based on human history "to more accurately reflect the varying grotesqueries of your (humanity's) nature".

## The Nightmare Orthography

Much like how The Teacher misunderstood humanity and attempted to placate them by offering paradise, a theory suggests that The Teacher misunderstood human history and defined the "grotesqueries" of human nature to imply the need for a hellish "Nightmare" Orthography.
